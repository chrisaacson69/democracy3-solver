Crises

Closest to flipping
Hysteresis, not a line. A crisis switches on above its start trigger and only switches off below a lower stop trigger. Between the two both states are self‑consistent, so the same country can sit in a good or a bad basin on identical policy. That gap is why a crisis is so much cheaper to cause than to clear — and why the headroom column above is worth watching before it reaches zero.

Dilemma pressure

state-driven only
Pressure, not a prediction. This is the deterministic half of each dilemma's trigger score, computed from the state you have built. The engine's firing threshold is not in the shipped data, so these rank which dilemmas your country is inviting — they do not say one will fire. The 26 dilemmas driven purely by a random roll are left out entirely.
Model parsed from the simulation CSVs shipped with Democracy 3 (Positech Games) — 97 nodes, 1,149 effect edges, 123 policies, 36 crises, 54 dilemmas. The equilibrium here is the same fixed point the project's Python solver computes, run in the browser; the two agree to 4×10−16. Six sources the network references but never defines (_effectivedebt_, _global_interest_rates_, the *_perc membership values) resolve to zero, exactly as in the solver — so debt and interest feedback are absent here too.
